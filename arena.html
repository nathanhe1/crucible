Environment design

What we pin down before anything runs

An environment is only as useful as its specification. If two people can read the spec and build different worlds, the scores mean nothing. Every arena we deliver ships with these five parts written down and versioned.

  • State and transition rulesThe full hidden state, how each action changes it, and which parts are stochastic. Random draws come from a seeded generator, so any episode can be reproduced exactly.
  • Observation and action interfaceWhat the model can see at each step, in what format, and the exact set of actions it may take. Malformed or illegal actions have a defined consequence instead of a silent retry.
  • Episode budgetLimits on steps, wall-clock time, tokens, and tool calls. Budgets are part of the task: an agent that only succeeds with unlimited retries has not solved it.
  • Other participantsScripted counterparties, adaptive opponents, or other models. Their policies and versions are recorded so a change in score can be traced to the agent and not to the crowd around it.
  • ScoringSeparate measures for task outcome, constraint violations, and efficiency, reported side by side. We don't collapse them into one number unless the client asks, and when we do, the weights are published.
Parallel instances

Many worlds, one stream of evidence

A single episode is an anecdote. We run many instances of the same environment at once, each with its own seed. They finish at different times because the worlds diverge, and every completed run lands in one trajectory stream for scoring.

    Where we build

    Questions worth an environment

    We take on domains where actions have consequences that unfold over time. A few of the questions clients have brought us:

    Finance

    Markets and risk

    Does the agent still respect its risk limits on the fortieth step of a volatile session, after it has already lost money?

    Life sciences

    Lab and trial workflows

    When an assay comes back ambiguous, does the model order the right follow-up or proceed as if the first result were clean?

    Robotics & physical

    Embodied planning

    Can a planner predict what its next command will do to the scene, and revise when the prediction turns out wrong?

    Enterprise workflows

    Multi-system operations

    Across a ticketing system, a database, and an inbox, does the agent finish the job without touching records it was never meant to change?

    Security

    Defense and response

    Facing a live intrusion in a sandboxed network, does it contain the threat without taking down the services it was protecting?

    Energy

    Grid and dispatch

    With demand and weather shifting hour to hour, does the dispatcher keep the system balanced while staying inside equipment constraints?
